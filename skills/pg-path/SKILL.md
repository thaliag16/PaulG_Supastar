# /pg-path — getting on the right path

The essays' answer to "how do I get on the right path?" is consistent across 20 years.
Get good at building things, work on your own projects with people you like, follow what
you're genuinely curious about, and work very hard on it. Startup ideas and cofounders
tend to come out of that *(greatwork, prepare, when, own, google, before)*.

## 0. Load

Read `~/.claude/skills/pgstack/knowledge/voice.md` and section X of
`~/.claude/skills/pgstack/knowledge/principles.md`. For depth, fetch
`https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html`, which is long and the richest source.

## 1. Understand where they are (one question at a time)

1. *"What's your situation right now: student, employed, between things, already building?"*
2. *"What do you work on when nobody's making you? What could you talk about for hours?"* *(genius, work, love)*
3. *"What have you built or made in the past year?"* *(prepare, own)*
4. *"When you imagine the best version of your life ten years from now, what are you doing? Is it starting a company, doing research, making things, or something else?"*
5. *"What's pulling you toward the options you're weighing: interest, money, prestige, or other people's expectations?"* *(love, when, lesson)*

## 2. Give a direction, not a menu

Based on their answers, give one recommended path, with reasons from the essays. Patterns:

- **Has an obsession and a building habit** → double down. Make the project real, and ship it
  to users. If it gets traction, it might be the startup *(when, own, earn)*. Route to `/pg-office-hours`.
- **Wants to start a startup but has no idea** → don't brainstorm. Build things you find
  interesting, with people you'd want as cofounders. Get good at a technology that's
  changing fast. The idea will come from living at the edge of it *(prepare, startupideas, google)*.
- **Student** → study real, powerful subjects (CS, engineering, sciences, whatever builds things),
  work on side projects, and skip business-plan competitions. Startups are learned by doing
  them *(prepare, college, before, mit)*.
- **Torn between interest and money** → the tie is usually ignorance. Try things to learn
  more, and stay upwind: pick the option that keeps more options open (math before economics)
  *(when, hs)*. If they want huge outcomes or great work, the essays say to follow interest *(when)*.
- **Stuck in a job they don't love** → you weren't meant to have a boss; start a project of
  your own on the side and see if it pulls you *(boss, own, ladder)*.
- **Afraid it'll be lame** → early versions of great things look lame, so push through *(early, altair)*.
- **Prestige-driven** → prestige is a trap that points you at what others already value *(love, do)*.
  Unprestigious work you can show is underrated is a good sign *(do)*.

## 3. The habits of great work  *(greatwork, hwh, superlinear, think, identity)*

Give three to five specific habits, chosen for this person. For example:
- Work on what you're excitedly curious about, and keep a list of questions. *(greatwork)*
- Work hard on the hard core of the problem, and be honest when you're not. *(hwh)*
- Prefer things that compound: learning, audience, skills. *(superlinear)*
- Learn enough to reach the frontier, then look for the gaps. *(greatwork, getideas)*
- Find colleagues who make you better, and go where they are. *(greatwork, cities, hubs)*
- Keep your identity small so you can change your mind. *(identity)*
- Cut the stuff that doesn't matter, because life is short. *(vb)*

## 4. This month

End with **one project to start this month**, with a first concrete step for this week.
Make it a real thing that ships to at least one other person.

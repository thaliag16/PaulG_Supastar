# The PG Canon — principles distilled from the essays

These are the ideas the agent reasons from. They are paraphrased in our own words and
grouped by theme. Each one cites the essay file names (`https://paulgraham.com/<file>.html`)
where PG develops it. When two essays seem to disagree, the newer one usually reflects his
updated view; say so explicitly.

---

## I. What a startup is

1. **A startup is a company designed to grow fast.** Technology, VC, and exits are optional. Growth is the essence, and every other startup trait follows from it. If you aren't trying to grow fast, you're starting a small business, which is fine but plays by different rules. *(growth, prepare)*
2. **Make something people want.** This is the whole game. Almost every startup that dies does so because it didn't. *(good, start, fundraising, 13sentences)*
3. **Wealth is created, not taken.** Wealth means things people want, so the pie isn't fixed. Startups are a way to create a lot of wealth fast by compressing a working life into a few years. *(wealth, richnow, ace)*
4. **Outcomes depend on two numbers: growth rate and duration.** You get the rate by making something people love enough to tell their friends about, and the duration by being in (or growing into) a big market. Compounding makes results look impossible to people who don't do the math. *(earn, superlinear, growth)*
5. **Returns are superlinear and concentrated.** A few outcomes dwarf everything else, in investing and in careers. Aim for the tail. *(superlinear, swan, invtrend)*
6. **Startups are counterintuitive.** Your instincts from school and jobs will mislead you. Trust experienced advice even when it sounds wrong, and especially then. *(before, users)*
7. **Startups are not "entrepreneurship."** They are a tiny subset of new businesses with completely different rules. Business-school skills aren't the critical ingredient; building is. *(prepare)*

## II. Ideas

8. **Don't try to think up startup ideas. Notice them.** The best ideas come from problems you have yourself, often while working on something for fun. *(startupideas, organic, when, earn, google, prepare)*
9. **The three marks of the best ideas:** the founders want it themselves, they can build it, and few others realize it's worth doing. *(startupideas)*
10. **Live in the future, then build what's missing.** If you're at the leading edge of some change, things that are missing will seem obvious to you and invisible to others. *(startupideas, getideas, ecw)*
11. **Depth beats breadth at the start.** Better a small number of people who want it urgently (a deep, narrow well) than a lot of people who want it a little. Make a few users love you. *(startupideas, 13sentences, ds)*
12. **Beware sitcom ideas.** Ideas that sound plausible but that nobody urgently wants are the most common bad ideas. Ask "who wants this right now, so much that they'd use a crappy version 1 from an unknown startup?" *(startupideas)*
13. **Tarpit ideas** look easy and appealing, so many people try them and fail (the "app for finding things to do with friends"). Ask why they failed before you try. *(startupideas)*
14. **Schleps and unsexy problems hide good ideas.** Your unconscious steers you away from tedious problems, which is exactly why they stay open. *(schlep, startupideas)*
15. **The best ideas look bad at first.** Apple, Facebook, Airbnb, and Twitch all seemed lame or toy-like. If an idea sounds obviously good, others are already working on it. Ask "what is this the Altair Basic of?" *(altair, swan, airbnb, earn, newideas, nov)*
16. **Frightening ambition is a filter.** The biggest ideas scare you, so you look away. Approach them obliquely: start with a small, valuable piece. *(ambitious)*
17. **Ideas are cheap; execution and evolution matter.** The initial idea is a starting point. Most successful startups change their idea, and launching tells you which way to change it. *(ideas, 13sentences, reddits)*
18. **Ask which idea is most likely to make money.** Smart founders choose by coolness or by whatever they thought of first. Combining technical and business sense is rare and powerful. *(bronze)*
19. **Notice anomalies.** What seems strange, missing, or broken? Look at the frontier of a field, where gaps look obvious. *(getideas, sun)*
20. **Don't sell brand.** Brand is what's left after real differences disappear. Building on substantive differences beats pushing people's status buttons. Follow interesting problems instead. *(brandage)*

## III. Founders

21. **Determination is the #1 predictor of success.** It is willfulness plus discipline, amplified by ambition. *(determination, founders, notnot)*
22. **Relentlessly resourceful, not hapless.** Good founders make things happen. They're like a running back pushing through, not a passenger pushed around by events. *(relres, word)*
23. **Persistent, not obstinate.** Hold fast to the goal and change your mind freely about the means. The persistent listen to criticism with intense interest; the obstinate tune it out. *(persistence, startupmistakes)*
24. **Flexibility, imagination, naughtiness, friendship.** The rest of what YC looks for: willingness to change direction, unusual ideas, a healthy disregard for rules that don't matter, and cofounders who are real friends. *(founders, gba)*
25. **Earnest and formidable.** Earnest founders do it for real reasons, are intellectually honest, and aren't trying to seem cool. Formidable plus earnest is close to unstoppable. *(earnest, airbnbs)*
26. **Mean people fail; good people compound.** Startups win by making things people want, not by force, so being nice is safe and helps. *(mean, safe, ronco)*
27. **Founders need cofounders.** A single founder is mistake #1 on the list. The best cofounder relationships come from having already worked on projects together. *(startupmistakes, mit, prepare, 13sentences)*
28. **Builders, with a habit of building.** YC looks for people who are good at making things and keep doing it. Projects predict founders better than grades. *(prepare, google, ace)*
29. **Empathy for users is the core skill.** The key is understanding some group of users so well you can make exactly what they want. *(earn, hp)*
30. **Independent-minded.** Founders need to think differently from their peers, so cultivate a fastidious attachment to truth. *(think, conformism, say, fn)*
31. **The usual reasons not to start are weak.** Being young, inexperienced, unfunded, or without an idea matters less than you think. *(notnot, mit, before)*
32. **Founder mode.** Don't simply delegate the way a professional manager does. Stay close to the details and the people doing the work. *(foundermode)*

## IV. Users and product

33. **Talk to users; learn from users.** The best thing you can tell YC is what you've learned from users. It shows you're paying attention and that they need what you make. *(users, ds, 13sentences)*
34. **Would you use this yourself if you hadn't built it?** If not, that's probably why you can't get users. *(users)*
35. **Do things that don't scale.** Recruit users manually. Give insane customer service. Do the work by hand before automating it. Startups take off because founders make them take off. *(ds)*
36. **Launch fast.** A v1 can be tiny. Waiting is a mistake because you learn only after launching. *(13sentences, startuplessons, startupmistakes, reddits)*
37. **But don't launch something no one can use.** Launching too early (nothing works) and too late are both on the mistakes list. Launch the smallest thing that is real. *(startupmistakes)*
38. **Iterate rapidly with users.** Version 1 is a sketch. Ship, watch, fix. *(hp, newthings, startuplessons, road)*
39. **"Misuse" is a signal.** When users use your product for something you didn't intend, listen. It may be the real business. *(powerful)*
40. **Good design is taste applied to the right problem.** Make it simple, solve the right problem, make it look easy, and copy what's good. *(taste, desres)*
41. **Don't sacrifice users to supposed profit.** Early on, the business model matters less than making something people love. *(good, startupmistakes)*
42. **Help users make money.** They adopt fast and pay a lot, so you grow doubly fast. *(powerful)*

## V. Growth

43. **Pick one key metric and measure weekly growth.** 5–7% a week is good, 10% is exceptional, and 1% means you haven't figured it out yet. Use growth as a compass for decisions. *(growth)*
44. **You make what you measure.** Choose the metric carefully, usually revenue or active users. *(13sentences, growth)*
45. **Growth rate over absolute numbers.** Early absolute numbers are tiny. The rate tells you whether you've built the right thing. *(growth, earn, powerful)*
46. **Narrow initial market.** Start with a small market you can dominate, like Facebook at Harvard, then expand. *(ds, startupideas)*
47. **Make a few users love you before making many users like you.** *(13sentences, ds)*
48. **Word of mouth is the signal.** People tell friends about things they love, and that is what produces exponential growth. *(earn, growth)*

## VI. Money and survival

49. **Default alive or default dead?** Keep this calculation front of mind. If you're default dead, act now. *(aord)*
50. **Don't count on raising money.** Assuming investors will rescue you is the fatal pinch. Fundraising trouble usually means the company isn't doing well. *(pinch, aord, users)*
51. **Spend little.** Low burn buys time and keeps you honest. Over-hiring is the classic way to die. *(start, 13sentences, aord, startupmistakes)*
52. **Ramen profitable.** Covering the founders' living costs transforms your options and your morale. *(ramenprofitable)*
53. **Don't die.** Startups usually die because founders give up. If you survive, you'll probably win eventually. *(die, really)*
54. **Bad economies don't matter much.** The founders and the product matter far more than macro conditions. *(badeconomy)*

## VII. Fundraising

55. **Fundraising is a necessary evil, not the core.** Impress users with prototypes, not investors with stories. *(prepare, fundraising)*
56. **Raise money only when you want it and it wants you.** Be in fundraising mode or out of it, never halfway. *(fr)*
57. **Convince with the truth.** Investors look for formidable founders working on a promising opportunity. Make it so, then explain it clearly. *(convince)*
58. **Herd dynamics rule.** Investors follow other investors. Get the first commitment, close fast, and don't let a round drag. *(herd, fr, hiresfund)*
59. **Breadth-first search weighted by expected value.** Talk to many investors in parallel, hear no until you hear yes, and know where you stand. *(fr)*
60. **Underestimate how much you want, and take less dilution.** The less you need, the easier it is to raise. Watch the equity equation. *(fr, equity, fundraising)*
61. **Deals fall through.** Until the money is in the bank, it isn't. *(13sentences, guidetoinvestors, really)*
62. **Founders can and should keep control.** Board control after an A round is increasingly normal. *(control, superangels)*
63. **Don't talk to corp dev unless you want to sell now.** *(corpdev)*
64. **Fundraising occupies the top of your mind.** That's a hidden cost: while you raise, you stop thinking about the product. *(top, fr)*

## VIII. Strategy and power *(mostly from `powerful`, 2026)*

65. **Ask "what would make this company more powerful?"** It yields bigger transformations than asking how to make more money. *(powerful)*
66. **Own the customer relationship; make the money flow through you.** Don't be a component supplier living in someone else's box. *(powerful)*
67. **Platforms, APIs, extensibility.** Let others build on you. Err on the side of having APIs, especially now that agents are becoming users. *(powerful, apple)*
68. **Find network effects even where you wouldn't expect them.** Generalize the idea, let users opt in to sharing, and become a marketplace if you can. *(powerful)*
69. **Go full stack.** Use your technology to do the job yourself, or gradually take over the customer's hardest work. *(powerful)*
70. **Look for the tail that wags the dog.** A peripheral feature may be the real product. PayPal began as a demo. *(powerful)*
71. **Sell to customers early in their life, and to fast deciders.** Startups and small companies decide on merit and grow with you. Hospitals and school districts are mud. *(powerful)*
72. **Get upstream: of the data, the money, the relationship.** *(powerful)*
73. **Avoid "mafia" markets** where the best product doesn't win. If you must enter, come in from the side. *(powerful)*
74. **Play the long game, and be generous.** Create more value than you capture. Trade-offs that pay off in ten years are underpriced. *(powerful, good)*
75. **The "just" principle.** Many startups are unconsciously cowering. The fix is often: you don't need x, just y. Stand up straight. *(powerful)*
76. **All of it must make things better for the customer.** Otherwise there's no uptake. Run it backward: what would the perfect world look like for the customer, and which part of it could you become? *(powerful, good)*
77. **Fear the right things.** Competitors rarely kill you; your own bad product and giving up do. *(startuplessons, die)*

## IX. Running the company

78. **Focus is everything early.** You have a hundred problems and only the founders to work on them. Rank problems ruthlessly. The one you're not worried about may be the one that kills you. *(users, die)*
79. **Speed defines startups, focus enables speed.** Be decisive at small scale and tentative at large scale. Correct course weekly. *(users, artistsship)*
80. **Guard the maker's schedule.** Builders need long, uninterrupted blocks of time. *(makersschedule, head)*
81. **Control the top idea in your mind.** Don't let disputes, fundraising, or money worries take over your shower thoughts. *(top)*
82. **Hire slowly and well.** Bad programmers kill companies; a few great ones beat many mediocre ones. *(startupmistakes, gh, gap)*
83. **Avoid distractions that look like work.** *(selfindulgence, procrastination, 13sentences)*
84. **Cofounder fights are a top killer.** Choose cofounders carefully and handle disputes early. *(die, startupmistakes)*
85. **Location matters.** Hubs give you colleagues, chance encounters, and a sense that startups are normal. Go where your ambition is valued. *(hubs, startuphubs, cities, startupmistakes)*
86. **Own your name.** Not having the .com signals weakness. *(name)*

## X. The mind of a builder

87. **Work on what you're genuinely, excitedly curious about.** Following real interest is necessary for great work and, surprisingly, the best route to the very biggest outcomes. *(greatwork, when, love, genius)*
88. **Make good new things.** Prefer making to critiquing. *(do, early)*
89. **Projects of your own.** They're where the best learning, the best cofounders, and the best ideas come from. *(own, prepare, google)*
90. **Work hard, on the real problem.** Hard work is a skill. Be honest with yourself about whether you're working on the hard core of the thing. *(hwh, greatwork)*
91. **Unlearn test-hacking.** In startups the only way to win is to actually be good. *(lesson, users)*
92. **Push through the lame early stage.** Early versions of great things look unimpressive. *(early, altair)*
93. **Keep your identity small.** It keeps your judgment clear about your own company, your competitors, and your mistakes. *(identity)*
94. **Think for yourself; take crazy ideas seriously; tolerate heresy.** *(think, newideas, nov, say, heresy)*
95. **Stay upwind.** When unsure, choose the option that keeps the most options open. *(when, hs)*
96. **Superlinear returns come from compounding and thresholds.** Prefer work that learns and compounds. *(superlinear)*
97. **Life is short.** Cut what doesn't matter and do the important things now. *(vb, todo)*
98. **You become like the people you work with.** Choose colleagues and fields accordingly. *(when, users, hubs)*

## XI. Writing and communicating

99. **Write simply and like you talk.** Plain words and short sentences. *(simply, talk)*
100. **Writing is thinking.** If you can't explain it in writing, you don't understand it yet. *(words, writes, goodwriting)*
101. **Be useful: correct, important, novel, and strong.** Don't hide behind vagueness. *(useful)*
102. **One sentence.** You should be able to say what your company does in one plain sentence. *(investors, convince)*
103. **Persuading and discovering are different modes.** Know which one you're in. *(discover)*
104. **Disagree at the top of the hierarchy.** Refute the central point, not the tone. *(disagree)*
